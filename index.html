About the Project

Underground utilities and tunnels are critical to modern cities, yet their condition is largely hidden from view. Maintenance decisions are often made with sparse, indirect and uncertain information about what lies beneath the surface.

M-Twin4US is a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowship developing a maintenance-oriented digital twin framework for buried infrastructure. The project combines ground-penetrating radar (GPR) sensing, Bayesian inversion and uncertainty quantification to turn subsurface measurements into decision-ready information for the maintenance of underground utilities.

Research Approach

1

GPR Sensing & Simulation

Ground-penetrating radar as the non-destructive sensing layer of the twin, supported by physics-based and surrogate GPR simulation.

2

Bayesian Inversion & UQ

Inferring subsurface conditions from GPR data while quantifying how certain—or uncertain—those estimates are.

3

Maintenance Decisions

Linking the digital twin to maintenance-oriented decision-making for underground utilities and tunnels.

Featured Output: GPR4Net

GPR4Net is a lightweight neural-network surrogate that predicts GPR B-scans in about 7 ms per sample, replacing the roughly 102-second FDTD simulation traditionally required. An interactive dashboard demonstrates three digital-twin scenarios.
